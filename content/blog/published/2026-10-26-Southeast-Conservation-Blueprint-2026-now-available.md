The latest update to the [Southeast Conservation Blueprint](/blueprint/), version 2026, is now available! Here are the major improvements for this year.

<!--more-->

**NEW INDICATORS**
- **Caribbean imperiled aquatic species** counts the number of aquatic Species of Greatest Conservation Need observed in each watershed. It matches the indicator already available for the continent and we’re super excited to finally have the data to do it. Big thanks to Jacob Laurain for collecting the new data in the U.S. Virgin Islands, Bonnie Myers and Gus Engman for connecting us with Puerto Rico data collected by NC Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit legend Tom Kwak and his team, and Kat Hoenke/the Southeast Aquatic Resources Partnership for being the connector for all things aquatic.
- **Gulf marine birds** identifies important areas for 24 species of marine birds. Big thanks to Jeff Gleason and Randy Wilson for help connecting with the big treasure trove of information from the Gulf of Mexico Marine Assessment Program for Protected Species (GoMMAPPS)

**MAJOR INDICATOR UPDATES**
- **Fire frequency** now has some big improvements thanks to suggestions from many of you. The previous version simply counted how many times an area had been burned. This new indicator now assesses how well fire-adapted areas match their historical fire regimes both now and in the recent past (1984-2024). It was tricky to combine all that info into a single understandable indicator. Huge thanks to the 19 people on the indicator review team that helped with that and reviewed multiple iterations of the indicator!
- **Imperiled reptiles & amphibians** is now updated to 2023 landcover and has an improved legend. It still identifies potential habitat to support herpetofauna Regional Species of Greatest Conservation Need (RSGCN) using rarity weighted richness.
- **Imperiled mammals** is also now updated to 2023 landcover with similar legend improvements. Like its herp analog, it also still identifies potential habitat to support mammal RSGCN using rarity weighted richness.

**INDICATOR OVERVIEWS**  
There’s now an [indicator page on the website](/blueprint-indicators/) with an overview of all Blueprint 2026 indicators.

**KNOWN ISSUES FIXED OR IMPROVED**  
Thanks to this year’s indicator improvements we fixed 6 known issues with the Blueprint and partially fixed 8 others. Those fixed or improved issues included: 
- Wetlands in Florida and Tennessee
- Grassland and savanna in Texas, Mississippi, Georgia, and Kentucky
- Terrestrial habitat in Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands
- Forest habitat in West Virginia
- Marine habitat off the coast of Florida and Louisiana
  
These are all very specific locations, based on workshops and user support, where we know the Blueprint priorities aren’t right. 

**IMPROVED PRIORITIZATION METHODS**
- The addition of Gulf marine birds required a small change to marine Zonation weighting methods. For this version, we did not increase the weight of the Gulf marine subregion to 1.0 even though it was slightly over the weight threshold (0.51). That improved consistency with the Atlantic subregion.

**IMPROVED CORRIDORS**
- The analysis now uses the new 2026 version of the Midwest Conservation Blueprint to improve the routes of corridors near the boundary between the Southeast and Midwest Conservation Blueprints.
- To address corridor routing issues in high and medium developed areas, the analysis now uses the new 2025 NLCD.

**EXPLORE THE DATA, LEARN MORE, AND GET HELP**  
You can explore the new data on the [Blueprint Explorer](https://apps.fws.gov/southeastblueprint/). The Explorer is a Google Maps-style interface that lets you quickly summarize and explore Blueprint information. This is also the interface that lets you upload shapefiles and get fancy reports with things like indicator tables/maps and information on sea-level rise and urban growth for your specific area.

The Blueprint data and documentation are also available on the [Blueprint page of the SECAS Atlas](https://secas-fws.hub.arcgis.com/pages/blueprint). The SECAS Atlas is an online mapping platform built in ArcGIS Hub. In the Atlas, you can download the data and documentation, view individual indicators, and make maps with Blueprint layers and other data. There’s even a [quick start map](https://fws.maps.arcgis.com/apps/mapviewer/index.html?webmap=15a8c484dd024e208a2981ad6d8478c2) that includes all the indicators and other underlying Blueprint data.  

We’re also happy to help if you’d just like help incorporating this latest version of the Blueprint in your work. You can [contact a member of the user support team](/staff/) and they’ll help you with things like making maps and combining Blueprint info with other local data and information you have.
